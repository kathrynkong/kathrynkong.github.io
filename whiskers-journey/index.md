## Design Question

<p class="wj-question">How might a small exploratory game place second-language vocabulary inside narrative and cultural contexts, rather than isolate it in drills or flashcards?</p>

A short narrative microgame created in Bitsy that explores how vocabulary and cultural knowledge can be embedded into playful, situated interactions rather than presented as isolated study items.

## Design Decisions

<div class="card-grid">

  <div class="card card--narrative">
    <h3>Contextual Vocabulary</h3>
    <p>Vocabulary isn't introduced as an isolated flashcard. Whisker finds a fish and "learns" the word 鱼 (yú) as part of that action, not a list memorized beforehand — the word belongs to an event, not a study set.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card card--research">
    <h3>Multilingual Scaffolding</h3>
    <p>Vocabulary appears through multiple linguistic cues at once — 牛奶 (niú nǎi – milk) — pairing Chinese characters, pinyin, and an English gloss, so players with different levels of familiarity with Chinese script can access the same interaction.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card card--creative">
    <h3>Culture Through Narrative</h3>
    <p>After drinking milk, Whisker gets an upset stomach and impulsively decides to embrace 中国茶文化 (Zhōngguó chá wénhuà – Chinese tea culture) instead — a joke that carries cultural context inside a character's voice and mishap, rather than as a factual aside.</p>
  </div>

</div>

## Reflection

Whisker's Journey was designed as a small-scale critical-making experiment rather than a full instructional game. Its limited scope made it possible to explore how movement, environmental interaction, bilingual text, humor, and cultural references could work together within a lightweight game engine.

The project became an early exploration of questions that now inform my broader research on game-based language learning: how game mechanics, narrative context, and instructional goals can be intentionally aligned.
